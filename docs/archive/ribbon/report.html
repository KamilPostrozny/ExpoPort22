The ribbon, redesigned

Three ways to show contextual actions over a terminal without being invisible, intrusive, or unreadable — and the measurement showing all three complaints are one bug.

14 agents · 5 literature sweeps · 6 mechanisms · 3 judges · 3 specs · 1 adversarial pass
Full specs: spec-accessory.md · spec-compact.md · spec-reader.md

Build Accessory — but ship the three-line gate first

Rotate the ribbon 90°: one 52pt opaque band where twelve caps and three caps occupy the identical footprint. It is the only candidate that stops drawing a column at all, which is the shape that was rejected. Before that, add a minimum-lifetime gate so the thing stops appearing for git log.

01The complaint is one bug

Three symptoms, one wrong decision — plus one wrong channel.

A translucent plate cannot serve two opposite extremes at once. Measured from src/ribbon.tsx as shipped, sRGB relative luminance, WCAG 2.2:

ElementMeasuredNeedsVerdict
12.5pt caption on surface@0.62, over bright output 1.76 : 14.5 : 1unreadable
Red destructive label, over bright output 1.69 : 14.5 : 1 the most dangerous cap is the least legible
Capsule body vs a normal dark pane 1.17 : 13 : 1invisible
The 5pt closed tab Lc ~15Lc 60 APCA defines Lc 15 as “treat as invisible”

The discovery cue is in the weakest channel there is. Bartram, Ware & Calvert (IJHCS 58(5), 2003) measured peripheral colour cues missed 25% of the time versus under 2% for motion, and ranked the motion families: blink < slow linear oscillation < zoom < travel. The shipped handle pulses opacity and scaleY at 0.53 Hz — a blink/zoom hybrid, the two worst families, on a 5pt colour target. It is close to a worst case on the published evidence.

Two more found by reading source rather than design notes:

  • The open panel's scrim kills the key bar. Its full-screen Pressable (ribbon.tsx:221) renders after the key bar (terminal.tsx:2376 vs :2320) with no zIndex in the tree — so while the panel is open, Ctrl / Esc / Tab / Paste / arrows / tabs are all dead. Combining a cap with a modifier is impossible today.
  • BarMenu and ClipboardPopover have the same defect, unfiled. Bare Glass over the live pane; they only get away with it because they open rarely.
Corrected constraint: the rule is not “never reserve vertical height”. Arming Ctrl already takes the bar stack from 60 to ~120pt, so the app already accepts a ~60pt terminal reflow on an explicit user action. The real rule is don't reserve height on a poll — and popBase is not a fixed anchor either.

02What the evidence says

Everything below was fetched and read, not recalled.

Legibility over content you don't control

No single colour can pass SC 1.4.11 over this terminal, because the terminal draws in the same theme — every candidate role colour can land on itself at exactly 1.00 : 1. The only construction that survives is W3C technique C40: two adjacent strokes of contrasting colours, so at least one always clears 3:1.

Apple's own answer to glass over bright content is not thinner glass — it is “a dark dimming layer of 35% opacity” and “thicker materials… provide better contrast for elements with fine features.” The Dynamic Island “uses a black opaque background.” Apple shipped this failure and reversed it: Liquid Glass went clear → frosted between iOS 26 beta 1 and beta 3. Meanwhile expo-blur on Android now needs the blurred content wrapped in BlurTargetView and cannot cross window boundaries — and our terminal is a WebView, so any translucent design was Android-broken from the start.

Being noticed without interrupting

  • Slow linear oscillation is the peer-reviewed best “appears by itself but isn't intrusive” signal; amplitude barely affects detection, so 2–4pt is enough.
  • WCAG 2.2.2 (Level A): motion that starts by itself and runs over five seconds must be stoppable. An indefinite withRepeat(…, -1) the user never started fails outright.
  • Yantis & Jonides (1990): attentional capture by abrupt onset is suppressed when attention is already focused. Someone reading output will not be captured by anything, at any amplitude — the cue must survive to their next glance. That one finding is why the 5pt tab could never have worked, and it is what separates the finalists.
  • Bailey & Konstan (2006, N=50): interrupting mid-task cost 3–27% more time, 2× the errors, 31–106% more annoyance — and deferring by just a few seconds largely mitigates it.

Reach, size, and how many items a menu can carry

  • Parhi et al. (MobileHCI '06): ≥9.2mm discrete / ≥9.6mm serial for one-handed thumb use; 29.9% error at 3.8mm, because thumbs land where a target looks, not where its hit rect is. Hit-slop on a 5pt tab was never going to work. 44pt ≈ 9.7mm at 3×.
  • Kurtenbach's thesis: breadth-12 flat menus “border on unreliability”; <10% error only to breadth 8 / depth 2. Material caps FAB menus at 2–6. The 12-cap agent recipe is over every documented ceiling — for a menu you aim at from memory. Which argues for a toolbar you read.
  • NN/g (n=179): hidden navigation used in 57% of mobile cases vs 86% for visible/combo, >20% lower discoverability, 15–39% longer tasks.

What the competition ships

  • Termius: the keyboard add-on “sits directly above the system keyboard and is always visible while typing.”
  • Blink Shell: SmartKeys live exactly as long as the on-screen keyboard; its Context Bar is summoned by double-tapping the iOS home bar.
  • Panic Prompt 3: an Extra Keyboard Row, shipped off by default.
  • Adobe's Contextual Task Bar — the closest analogue — has as its top complaint that it repositions itself mid-task. Adobe's shipped answer was a pin.

Every shipping iOS terminal puts extra keys in a horizontal band in the bottom chrome. None of them reserves it permanently.

Platform escape hatches, checked

  • @expo/ui (~57.0.9, installed, unused) gives a real SwiftUI Menu / Compose DropdownMenu — but a UIMenu cannot present itself. Programmatic open is Android-only. It can be the open half, never the whole design.
  • expo-glass-effect (~57.0.1, installed, unused) is iOS 26+ only.
  • RNGH will not arbitrate against system edge gestures (issue #833, closed as not planned), and Android's back gesture owns both edges.
  • The repo has zero accessibility code today — no accessibilityLabel, no accessibilityRole, no useReducedMotion anywhere in src/.

03Six mechanisms, three judges

Each judge saw one axis only, and scored blind to the others.

CandidateDiscoverLegible BuildableTotal
Accessory — rotate the ribbon 90° 98.57 24.5
Compact — a Live Activity pill 87.58 23.5
Reader — the ⋯ key becomes the recipe 499 22
Clearing — park in the emptiest band of text 765 18
Native — a real SwiftUI / Compose menu 394 16
Flick — press the edge, a radial blooms 653 14

The three that died, and the lesson each left behind:

  • Clearing scans the pane for empty rows — but four of six recipes (vim, pager, htop, agent) are alt-screen apps that fill the pane edge to edge. There is no empty band, so it freezes an opaque, touch-eating rectangle mid-pane for the life of the process.
  • Native cannot self-appear, which is the feature's defining requirement. It is half a design by construction, and cannot carry a live timer or the two-tap arm.
  • Flick needs a hot-zone overlapping both Android's predictive-back edge and the terminal's own pan recogniser. A right-thumb scroll near the edge would dim the screen and could fire a stray ^C into a running job.

04The three approaches

Each was specced in full, then attacked by a verifier whose default was that it was wrong.

recommended 24.5 / 30 ~1 day 5 files 0 new deps

Accessory — rotate the ribbon 90°

One 52pt opaque band pinned at popBase. At rest, a 44pt identity chip flush to the trailing edge: glyph, process name, live clock. Tap and it unrolls leftward into a horizontal row of 44pt caps. A 12-cap agent recipe occupies the identical footprint to a 3-cap running recipe, forever.

Why it gets noticed

Two channels, neither behind a gesture. The 44pt chip — and, stronger, the caps sit in the strip the eye already scans for Ctrl/Esc/Tab whenever the keyboard is up. It sidesteps Yantis & Jonides instead of fighting it: the cue is found on a glance the user is already making.

Why it's readable

Opaque theme.panel, no glass, no blur — every contrast figure becomes a constant. foreground on panel = 12.13 : 1 on Mocha. Danger caps render bold because Latte's red is 4.46:1 (fails regular, passes the 3:1 bold floor).

Why it scales

12 caps as a column is 660pt — taller than the safe area, which is what forces today's maxCapsHeight clamp. Rotated, it is 972pt of horizontal content in a band that is 52pt tall for every recipe. The clamp is deleted, not tuned.

Honest cost

Caps past the fourth need a flick on the agent recipe. The open band is the widest surface of the three, and the resting chip covers a corner permanently. With the chord strip up, bottom chrome stacks to ~172pt.

Both risky bets have in-repo precedent that was verified, not assumed: the animated width inside a clip already ships in NamePill (keybar.tsx:938), and the opaque plate is the finished version of the surface@0.62 emergency patch the codebase already recorded as insufficient. It fixes the key-bar scrim bug for free.

did not survive 23.5 / 30 smallest diff

Compact — the handle as a Live Activity pill

The Dynamic Island's minimal presentation: a 44pt opaque plate carrying glyph, name and clock, announcing itself once with six finite cycles of a 4pt nudge, then still. Tap and it grows in place into a 208pt panel whose bottom header row is the pill. Twelve caps are split by a three-segment control so no column exceeds five rows.

It has the best-argued contrast reasoning of the three — a worked C40 floor of 3.58:1 against any luminance whatsoever — and it is the smallest delta from shipping code. It still failed verification, because the rejection was of the shape, not the material: this is still a column of capsules climbing over the output from a small resting token, and its own first trade-off concedes it “will be read as ‘the same thing again’”. Its 208 × 342pt panel is the largest persistent-over-output surface of the three. Its motion table and C40 arithmetic should be harvested into whatever ships.

22 / 30 cheapest build net deletion zero occlusion

Reader — the ⋯ key becomes the recipe

Delete the floating layer. Detection's whole ambient expression is the key bar's ⋯ circle filling with the recipe's identity colour and swapping its glyph — a shape-and-fill change on a 49pt control at a fixed, already-visited location. The caps become the first section of the menu that key already opens.

What it wins

No new gesture, no new layer, no new back-handler rung, no edge-gesture collision on either platform, zero occlusion at rest. It is the only candidate that is less code than today — ribbon.tsx's 304 lines go away.

What it can't promise

That anyone notices. By its own cited literature, a 49pt disc changing fill on a control the eye has learned to ignore, against a pane full of masking transients, is the textbook change-blindness stimulus. And ⋯ sits on the left — the far side from a right thumb.

Twelve caps also make the menu 810pt tall, pushing Upload and Settings below the fold whenever an agent is running — an IA inversion the spec names but does not solve.

05Corrections from verification

The adversarial pass re-read the source and node_modules. These override the spec bodies.

  1. All three specs repeat a false claim. “Reanimated 4 neuters withRepeat under Reduce Motion, leaving the handle a static invisible 5pt bar.” Traced in node_modules: each withTiming jumps to its toValue and withRepeat stops after one rep. Since the breath is seeded at 1 and the sequence ends on withTiming(1), it resolves to opacity: 0.95 — fully visible and still. Reduce Motion currently makes the handle brighter. The real defect is the WCAG 2.2.2 violation for everyone else, which stands.
  2. Don't hand-roll useReduceMotion() — Reanimated 4.5.1 already exports useReducedMotion. Only Compact got this right.
  3. theme.muted is unsafe on the generated schemes. On Solarized Dark it is 2.96 : 1 on surface — Compact's claimed ≈4.6:1 floor is wrong and would ship a failing caption. Use rgba(theme.foreground, 0.78) and drop theme.muted from the design.
  4. Accessory's C40 numbers don't reproduce. The strokes composite against the background; the crossover is near L ≈ 0.165 where both sit at ≈4.2:1 — better than stated, but the stated figures are wrong.
  5. Accessory contradicts itself on the destructive cap — “off-screen until scrolled to” vs its own mockup showing it first at x = 0. Hiding it means reordering ribbon-recipes.ts, contradicting the “zero recipe changes” claim.
  6. Reader's inkOn helper inverts on its own motivating example — on Latte's grey dot it returns the 2.3:1 option by one unit. Drop the heuristic; pick by theme.isDark.
  7. The Safari Reader precedent is overstated. iOS 18 shows the words “Reader Available”; macOS adds a new button. The real precedent argues for a worded cue in existing chrome.
  8. Citations: Parhi's ≥9.2mm is discrete and ≥9.6mm is serial — “≥9.6mm one-handed” as a separate finding doesn't exist. The CHI '25 signifiers paper is Mackamul, Chevalier, Casiez & Malacria; Bailly is not an author.

06The question nobody asked

Every spec tunes when within a process the ribbon arrives. None asks whether the process qualifies.

The running recipe matches any non-shell foreground command. It fires for git log, ls, npm test, every rg — dozens of times an hour. That is why an unrequested surface reads as intrusive no matter how well it is drawn.

A minimum-lifetime gate in ribbon-model.ts — show running only once the process has been alive ~3s, which is exactly when kill / bg / stop become useful — is a three-line pure change with a unit test in a suite that already exists. It would do more for “not intrusive” than any amount of plate, blur or easing, and it composes with all three designs.

The stronger version of the same thought: make it pull, not push. If long-pressing ⋯ summons the recipe on demand, the whole “it appears by itself and must not interrupt” constraint disappears. That deserved to be one of the six and wasn't.

07Order of work

Cheapest and most decisive first.

  1. The running minimum-lifetime gate in ribbon-model.ts, plus its unit test. Three lines. Ship it alone and see whether the complaint changes.
  2. Spike @expo/ui's Menu on both platforms with a screenshot — the cheapest high-value verification available, and what decides whether the open half can ever be a platform menu.
  3. Build Accessory, with the §05 corrections applied and theme.muted dropped from the design.
  4. Run the adversarial contrast test: htop on a many-core box, bat CLAUDE.md, Latte at outdoor brightness. Sample the worst pixels along the perimeter and assert the ratio. The current design was never measured against anything harder than an idle prompt — which is exactly how it shipped at 1.69 : 1.

They are not mutually exclusive. Reader's ⋯-circle fill is ~15 lines and composes with Accessory. If the resting chip's corner occlusion becomes the new complaint, that pairing — loud identity in the chrome, band summoned on demand — is the fallback, and it is the “pull, not push” design in disguise.

The Android emulator harness on this box means every Android divergence these specs concede — no swipe, edge-gesture collisions, no blur — is testable today rather than assumed.
